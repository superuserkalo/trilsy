# Copy playbook (Phase A, session 01a)

A study of how Wispr Flow's notetaker page writes, with Superwhisper, Granola, Otter, Fireflies and others as comparison. Every quote was read from the live page on 2026-10-13; full text is in `refs/text/`. The rules and rewrites at the end are drafts for the Phase B copy deck. Nothing here is approved site copy.

## 1. How the H1 states the outcome

The strong ones are a noun phrase for the thing, then a short clause for the result.

- Wispr Notetaker: "Meeting notes that get the details right." Seven words. Subject is the output (notes), not the product. The clause names one quality, accuracy, and the whole page then proves that one quality.
- Granola: "The AI notepad for back-to-back meetings". Category plus the person's situation.
- MacWhisper: "The all-in-one Mac app to transcribe it all." Platform, category, verb.
- Superwhisper: "Just speak. Write faster." Two imperatives, action then result.
- Wispr Flow: "Don't type, just speak."

The weak ones name a rank or coin a term: "The #1 AI Assistant For Your Meetings" (Fireflies), "Your AI notetaker is now also your Conversational Knowledge Engine" (Otter), "AI notetaking that is out of this world" (Fathom), "The meeting intelligence platform you can trust." (Jamie).

Structure to borrow: **[the output or the thing] + [that / for / to] + [one concrete result]**, in present tense, with a full stop.

## 2. How the subhead adds who and why

The subhead carries what the H1 left out: the mechanism, the audience, or the difference.

- Granola: "Notes, actions and memory. Without a meeting bot." Three outputs, then the difference in four words.
- Wispr Flow: "The voice-to-text AI that turns speech into clear, polished writing in every app." Category, transformation, scope.
- Superwhisper: "Turn your voice into polished text. Works in Slack, Gmail and any other site or app." Transformation, then scope as two examples plus "any other".
- Fathom: "Fathom summarizes your meetings so you can focus on the conversation. Now available bot-free."
- Wispr Notetaker has no subhead. The animated transcript does that job, and the first sentence after the hero completes it: "Wispr Notetaker captures the hard-to-spell words and easy-to-miss details."

None of the strong ones names an audience by job title in the hero. The audience is implied by the situation ("back-to-back meetings") or left open.

## 3. Voice

- **Person:** second person throughout. "Fits into your workflow.", "Your meetings are yours.", "Your voice stays yours." The product is named or is "it"; "we" is rare and reserved for the company speaking plainly ("Our switching guide walks you through the process").
- **Tense:** present, active. "One button joins and records. Catches back-to-backs and calls that aren't on your calendar."
- **Sentence length:** short, often a pair where the second sentence turns the first. "Fits into your workflow. Doesn't change it." "Switch without losing a thing." Body lines run 8 to 18 words.
- **Fragments are allowed** when the subject is obvious: "Catches back-to-backs…", "Both in one subscription."
- **Humor:** dry and small, never in the claim. Wispr: "Google Meet, Teams, Huddles, lunch catchups. If words are being said, Notetaker captures them." Granola's cookie notice: "OK with Cookies? Not the granola kind." Superwhisper: "No Wi-Fi, no problem."
- **What Wispr never says on this page:** "AI-powered", "revolutionary", "seamless" in its own voice (it appears only inside a customer quote), "#1", "best". It never describes the technology. It describes what gets written down.

## 4. Section headings and the body under them

Wispr's headings are outcome statements addressed to the reader, sometimes a two-beat pair:

- "Wispr Notetaker captures the hard-to-spell words and easy-to-miss details."
- "So you can answer the questions, write the follow-up, and keep things moving."
- "Fits into your workflow. Doesn't change it."
- "Switch without losing a thing."
- "Bring Notetaker to your team."
- "You might be wondering." (FAQ)
- "Put your words to work in more ways than one." (closing)

Under each, a label of two or three words and one or two lines that add a concrete detail:

- "Starts automatically" / "One button joins and records. Catches back-to-backs and calls that aren't on your calendar."
- "Whatever you use" / "Google Meet, Teams, Huddles, lunch catchups. If words are being said, Notetaker captures them."
- "Connects with your AI" / "Notetaker plugs into Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and any tool that speaks MCP."

Granola does the same with a human-first heading: "Humans in the room, not bots" / "Granola doesn't join your meeting. Instead, it transcribes in the background while you stay present". And: "Give your full attention" / "Don't choose between listening and taking good notes."

Compare Fireflies, which labels features in Title Case and restates the label in the body: "Speaker Recognition" / "Fireflies identifies different speakers in meetings and audio files."

## 5. How they answer the four questions

**Does it join my call?**

- Granola puts it in the subhead ("Without a meeting bot.") and explains the mechanism in the strip: "Uses your computer audio, so doesn't invite a bot".
- Jamie makes it a hero fact, "No bot joins your call", then a section: "Jamie runs on your device without a bot joining your calls."
- Wispr folds it into the tools answer: "with no integrations to set up and no bot to invite."
- Wispr also states the consequence honestly, which no one else does: "Notetaker captures audio locally on your device rather than joining calls as a visible bot, so participants may not receive an automatic notification that transcription is taking place. You are responsible for informing everyone before you begin."

**Is it private?**

- Wispr opens with a plain sentence, then specifics: "Your meetings are yours." … "Notetaker does not create voiceprints or biometric profiles of you or anyone on your calls. Your content is never sold and is never used to train AI models without your consent. You can delete any meeting, transcript, or note at any time." It also admits where audio goes: "stored only temporarily on your device and, in some cases, in secure cloud storage."
- MacWhisper is the model for a local product: "Process sensitive content locally without data ever leaving your Mac."
- Jamie: "Your meetings are nobody else's business."
- Granola: "Private by default, easy to share if you choose".
- Fireflies and Fathom answer with badge lists ("GDPR, SOC2, More"; "SOC 2 Type II | GDPR | HIPAA Compliant | SSO / SCIM"). That says nothing a person can picture.

**What does it cost?**

- Wispr: "Notetaker is included with Wispr Flow, and Flow's free plan is free forever. Upgrade to Pro for unlimited use…"
- Granola states the free tier and its exact edge: "Unlimited meeting notes for free" / "Take as many notes as you'd like. Upgrade to view and work with notes older than 30 days."
- Superwhisper: "Yes, you can try the Pro features for 3,000 words for free, after that the free tier features are available to you forever." Its pricing cards carry "40% off for Students" and "30 day refund available for all plans".
- Fathom puts it in the button: "GET STARTED - FREE FOREVER".

**Does it work with my tools?**

- Wispr states a principle, then examples: "If your computer can play it, Notetaker can capture it. Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, Discord, or a call in your browser".
- Granola: "Works with Zoom, Google Meet, Teams and every other meeting app."
- Superwhisper: "Works in Slack, Gmail and any other site or app."
- For in-person, Wispr gives a usable instruction: "set your laptop in the middle of the table."

The pattern for all four: answer in the first two or three words ("Yes.", "No.", "Your meetings are yours."), give the mechanism in one sentence, and state the limit if there is one.

## 6. CTA wording and microcopy

- **Buttons** name the action and the price: "Get started for free", "Download for free" (Wispr); "Download for free" (Granola); "Download for Mac" (Superwhisper); "Get Pro"; "Contact us".
- **Helper text under buttons** is one factual line: "Available on Mac and Windows" (Wispr); "Available for macOS, Windows, iOS, Android" (Granola); "Also available for iOS and Android" (Superwhisper); "We'll get back to you within 24 hours" (Superwhisper, under "Contact us").
- **In-mockup states** are written like the real app: "Transcribing" and "Enhancing notes" (Granola), "Write notes..." as the empty note field (Granola), "Cleaning up..." (Wispr Flow), "Zoom Meeting Detected" with a "Record" button (MacWhisper).
- **Mockup content is specific and slightly messy:** "Q3 messaging rollout, are teams ready??" (Granola), "okay I just shared my screen can everyone see it" (Wispr).
- **FAQ answers** start with the answer. "Yes!" / "If a meeting is not on your calendar, Notetaker will detect that you are in a call and ask if you want to start taking notes." "Notetaker runs on Mac and Windows. Mobile is on the roadmap."
- **FAQ answers admit limits:** "More languages are coming soon. You're welcome to try it on meetings in other languages, but expect mixed results until dedicated support arrives." Superwhisper: "Intel Macs work best with Cloud models. Offline models only run really well on Apple Silicon macs."

## 7. Specific claims against vague ones

| Specific | Vague |
| --- | --- |
| "names like "Aditya" are spelled correctly and product names stay intact" (Wispr) | "Fireflies is the industry leader in transcription accuracy." |
| "Notetaker transcription supports 21 languages: English, Spanish, French…" (Wispr) | "Transcribe meetings in English, Spanish, French, & several others." (Fireflies) |
| "Upgrade to view and work with notes older than 30 days." (Granola) | "Advanced tools for a refined workflow." (Superwhisper) |
| "Granola names speakers only on Google Meet and in Zoom's Mac app, and shows "Me" and "Them" everywhere else." (Wispr, about a competitor) | "Otter is built with the security, governance, and compliance controls enterprises expect." |
| "Keyboard" at "45 wpm" beside "Flow" at "220 wpm" (Wispr Flow) | "Powerful features, seamlessly integrated." (Superwhisper) |
| "Audio deleted after transcription. Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2) and at rest (AES 256)." (Jamie) | "Seamless in the background, with privacy at its core" (Jamie) |

The specific ones work for three reasons. A reader can check them. They contain a noun the reader can picture (a name, a file, a number of days). And a limit stated plainly makes the claim next to it believable. Wispr's strongest move is an invitation to test: "The best way to see the difference is to record one meeting with Notetaker alongside your current tool and compare the transcripts."

## Copy rules for Trilsy

1. **The headline says what Trilsy is in plain nouns.** A conversation word, "notes", and enough to place it on a Mac. No metaphor, no mood line. Nothing sits above it.
2. **The subhead gives the mechanism in one sentence and ends on the difference.** It records, it transcribes, it writes the notes; no bot joins.
3. **Speak to "you", in the present tense.** Sentences stay under about 16 words. "Trilsy" or "it" for the app; "we" only when the makers are speaking, such as about what is not built yet.
4. **Name the range as a principle with a few examples, never as a closed list.** "Any call, lecture or interview", "anything your Mac can play". App names appear only as examples followed by "and anything else".
5. **Say exactly where things live.** Audio is saved on your Mac. For transcription and notes, state only what ships today, and never word it so that on-device or API sounds like the only way. This rule is rewritten after B8.
6. **Headings are an outcome or the visitor's own question.** The line under a heading adds one concrete detail: a file format, a step, a place. It never restates the heading.
7. **Specific beats clever.** "Markdown, text or subtitles" instead of "export anywhere". "Apple silicon, macOS 15 or later" instead of "modern Macs". Use a number only when it is true and checkable.
8. **Play is a small aside, at most one per section, and never inside a claim.** The warmth comes from sounding like a person who was in the room. The characters and motion carry the rest.
9. **Words we do not use:** AI-powered, seamless, effortless, magic, supercharge, unlock, #1, best, revolutionary, "for students" in the hero, and any badge or certification.
10. **Tell the truth about the state of things.** No "Download" button until a signed installer exists. Open source and on GitHub is a fact we can lead with. Say plainly that people should tell others before recording them. Prices and anything unconfirmed carry `TODO: confirm`.

## Before and after

These are drafts to react to. Where two options are given, the first is the recommendation. The owner decides in Phase B.

**1. Hero headline**

- Before: "Meeting notes, written by your Mac."
- Problem: "Meeting" narrows Trilsy to work calls, against the universal positioning. "Written by your Mac" will read as on-device only, which the product facts say never to imply.
- After (recommended): "Notes for every call, lecture and interview."
- After (alternative): "Your Mac takes the notes."
- Why: the first passes the five-second test alone and names the three conversations from the product facts. The second is warmer and shorter but needs the subhead to say notes of what.

**2. Hero subhead**

- Before (live site): "Your conversations, beautifully kept. A private AI notetaker that stays on your Mac."
- Problem: "beautifully kept" is unprovable, and nothing says how it works or that no bot joins.
- After: "Trilsy records what you say and what your Mac plays, transcribes it and writes notes you can edit. No bot joins the call."

**3. Strip item: "No bot in the call"**

- After: "No bot joins. Trilsy records from your Mac."
- Why: gives the mechanism, as Granola does with "Uses your computer audio, so doesn't invite a bot".

**4. Strip item: "Works with every app"**

- After: "Works with anything your Mac can play, and with the room you are in."
- Why: "every app" is a claim someone will test against an edge case. A principle is true by construction and covers in-person recording.

**5. Strip item: "Recordings stay on your Mac"**

- After: "Your audio is saved on your Mac."
- Why: this is true today and stays true if API processing is offered later. "Stay" promises more than the intended product may keep. Revisit after B8.

**6. Strip item: "Notes you can edit and keep"**

- After: "Edit, search and export as Markdown, text or subtitles."
- Why: three verbs and three formats, all from the product facts.

**7. Steps: "Record / Transcribe / Notes"**

- Before: three bare nouns and verbs that do not match each other.
- After, as label and line:
  - "Press record." Trilsy captures your microphone and your Mac's sound together.
  - "Get the transcript." Everything that was said, in order, with timestamps. `TODO: confirm` that timestamps show in the transcript view.
  - "Read the notes." An overview first, then the detail section by section.
- Why: each label is something the person does or gets. The third line states how the app really writes notes (per section, then an overview).

**8. Live hero headline (not in the brief's list, but it is what the site says today)**

- Before: "Be here. We'll take the notes."
- Problem: a stranger cannot tell this is a Mac app or what "here" is. It could belong to any notetaker.
- After: covered by item 1.

**9. Live eyebrow and section heading**

- Before: "Made for the way you think" above "All the details. None of the busywork."
- Problem: the eyebrow breaks the nothing-above-a-headline rule in spirit and says nothing. "Busywork" is borrowed SaaS voice.
- After: "The overview, and everything behind it." with the line "Short notes for catching up. Full sections when you need to check what was said."
