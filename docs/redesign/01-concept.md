### Concept 1: The table (`prototypes/b2-concept-1.html`, `b2-concept-1-1440.png`)

- **Headline:** "Notes for every call, lecture and interview."
- **Composition:** centred text, then a wide hand-drawn scene. Four people at a long table, two each side, with the product window standing between them like a screen on the table. Their speech bubbles point into the window.
- **Storyboard (14s loop):**
  1. 0.0s: window titled "Product sync" at 00:00, flat waveform, empty transcript; people at rest.
  2. 0.6s: the left speaker's mouth opens and her bubble draws in ("Can we move the launch to the 14th?").
  3. 2.0s: the dashed arrow animates from bubble to window, the waveform moves, transcript line 1 types in under a "Priya" chip.
  4. 4.5s: the right speaker answers; lines 2 and 3 follow, 2s apart.
  5. 8.0s: "Create notes" presses (400ms), and the notes column fills in order: Overview, Decisions, Actions.
  6. 11.5s: hold 2s, then bubbles and text fade out together (500ms) and it loops. Reduced motion shows the final frame.
- **Logo row:** full width directly under the table, at the fold.
- **Why it can be read fastest:** the biggest characters and the clearest cause and effect (people talk, words arrive). Nothing needs a click or a tab.
- **Cost:** it shows one setting, a work meeting, and leans on the headline for lectures and interviews. The table and four people take most of the first viewport, so the window is a medium-sized object.

### Concept 2: One window, three settings (`prototypes/b2-concept-2.html`, `b2-concept-2-1440.png`) — recommended

- **Headline:** "Any conversation in. Clean notes out."
- **Composition:** headline, subhead and CTA on the left (the Notion layout), one framed scene on the right. Three tabs on the scene: Call, Lecture, Interview. The product window is fixed in the lower part of the frame; the hand-drawn setting above it changes.
- **Storyboard (about 9s per setting, 27s loop):**
  1. 0.0s: the Call setting is drawn: a person at a laptop, two video tiles; the active tab's underline starts to fill; the window reads 00:00 and is empty.
  2. 0.6s: she speaks; her bubble draws in; the dashed arrow carries the words down to transcript line 1.
  3. 2.5s to 5.5s: two more lines arrive, one per speaker; the waveform follows the voices.
  4. 6.0s: "Create notes" presses and the notes assemble (Overview, Decisions, Actions).
  5. 8.5s: the tab underline completes; the scene behind the window swaps in 500ms to the Lecture setting (a presenter at a board, students in the foreground) while the window swaps to "Biology 201, lecture 9" and clears; the cycle repeats for Interview, then back to Call.
  6. A visitor can click any tab; that stops the cycle on their choice. Reduced motion: no cycle, Call shown, tabs still work.
- **Logo row:** left column, directly under the CTA.
- **Why I recommend it:** it is the only one that shows the three words in the positioning sentence being true. The product window is whole and large in the first viewport (concepts 1 and 3 show it smaller or cropped). The window staying fixed while the world around it changes tells a stranger "same product, any conversation" without a sentence. Tabs are a pattern we have a precedent for (Amplemarket). The logo row sits in a quiet column rather than competing with the picture.
- **Cost:** at any moment two of the three settings are hidden. The hero scene and window together need three sets of content and art. The framed card with a hard shadow is the heaviest graphic treatment of the three.

### Concept 3: The sticker and the page (`prototypes/b2-concept-3.html`, `b2-concept-3-1440.png`)

- **Headline:** "Be in the conversation. Trilsy writes the notes." (close to the live "Be here. We'll take the notes.", which the owner treats as baseline copy)
- **Composition:** centred and editorial. A sticker-style pair of characters in an interview sit above the headline. The product window rises from the bottom edge, cropped by the fold.
- **Storyboard (12s loop, then the scroll takes over):**
  1. 0.0s: the window slides up from the fold to its resting place (500ms) and shows 00:00.
  2. 0.8s: the guest's bubble types its line ("We moved the whole team to Lisbon in March.").
  3. 2.5s: a dashed ribbon draws from the bubble down into the window; transcript line 1 appears.
  4. 5.0s: the interviewer asks; line 2. The guest answers; line 3.
  5. 9.0s: the notes appear in the lower half of the window, below the fold. A small hint of them is visible.
  6. On scroll the window rises to fill the screen and the notes complete (the hero-to-stage idea from the 01b study, not copied from it).
- **Logo row:** full width under the cropped window, at the fold.
- **Why it might win:** the most editorial and the most like the owner's second reference (a sticker above a serif headline). One pair of characters is the cheapest to draw and animate.
- **Cost:** only about 140px of the window shows in the first viewport, so a stranger sees the characters and headline but not the product working until they scroll. It is the weakest on "knows what it is within seconds". A seated interview also implies one setting.

### Illustration sourcing (recommendation: a)

- **(a) Original SVG characters, drawn in one style (recommended).** The previews are made this way: one set of rules (ink line with uneven edge, flat fill nudged off the line, one outline colour, shared proportions, seven hair styles, four skin tones, glasses and gestures as options). Same line weight everywhere is guaranteed because it is one code path, and the same characters can drive the logo mark (B6), 404 and use-case art. No licence to track. Honest limit: this is code-drawn hand-drawn, so it reads warm and consistent but not like a real illustrator's line. If the owner wants a truer hand, option (c) improves on it.
- **(b) A permissive set.** Open Peeps and Humaaans (Pablo Stanley) are published as CC0 and are mix-and-match people, so they are diverse and free to use commercially without attribution. I would check the licence on the day we adopt one. The cost is that they are generic, used on many sites, and do not match our logo or the product window; their poses would not fit "talking into a window".
- **(c) A commissioned set.** Best final quality and ownership, but needs the owner and time. It can follow (a): the previews and the kit then act as the brief for the illustrator.

### Animation approach (recommendation: Motion with inline SVG)

Motion is already in the stack. The characters are a few dozen paths each, so SVG with Motion handles mouth swaps, bubble draw-ins and dashed-arrow travel in a few kilobytes and one dependency, and the transcript and notes are ordinary DOM text, so they stay readable, selectable and crawlable. Lottie would add a runtime and bake the text into pixels. Rive is the best for a character that reacts (a face that follows the speaker) and costs a runtime plus a design tool; it is a later upgrade for one character if we want it, not a launch need. One easing token, 400 to 600ms, throughout.

## B5. Motion plan (drafted 2026-10-13)

**Rules.** Every animation explains the product, guides attention or confirms an action. One easing token for the whole site, `--ease-trilsy: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (fast start, soft landing), with two durations on it: 400ms for small confirmations (a button press, a tab change, a chip appearing) and 600ms for scene changes (a bubble drawing in, a setting swap). Nothing outside 400 to 600ms except the product demo's own typing and the 70s marquee. No decorative parallax, cursor effects, floating blobs, bouncy springs, or staggered fade-up on everything. Reduced motion: the hero shows the final frame of its current setting and the tabs still switch with an instant change; the marquee is a static wrapped row; the pinned story becomes stacked cards. Motion library for DOM and SVG; the photograph swap is a vgpu shader in Phase C with an instant swap as the fallback.

**Hero storyboard** (lecture first; Meeting and Interview run the same six beats with their own content; about 11s per setting, a 33s loop, calm). The scene is the people, their bubbles and the window; scroll plays no part in it.
1. 0.0s, the setting is on screen: figures standing, the window titled with the setting, 00:00, a flat waveform, an empty transcript. Nothing moves except the cursor caret.
2. 0.6s, the speaker's bubble draws in (600ms, scale from 0.96 and opacity, anchored at its tail on the speaker's head). Their name chip appears with it. This tells a visitor who is talking.
3. 2.0s, the words travel: the bubble's text fades while the same line types into the transcript under the speaker's matching chip (about 1.4s at reading pace), and the waveform bars start to move with the voice. This shows speech becoming a transcript, with no arrow needed.
4. 4.5s, the second speaker's bubble draws in; their line follows, then a third line. In the lecture the student with the hand up asks, in the meeting the listener replies.
5. 8.0s, "Create notes" presses (400ms, a small depress and release), and the notes column fills in order: Overview, then the second list, then the checklist, each 400ms, 200ms apart. This is the product's whole job in one beat.
6. 10.2s, hold; at 10.6s the active tab's accent underline completes, and the next setting swaps in 600ms (people and bubbles cross-fade, the window title and content change). A visitor can click a tab at any time; that stops the cycle on their choice.

**Pinned story: record, transcribe, notes** (home section 3; the photograph effect from the `01b` study, designed here for Trilsy, not copied). - On paper the colour pools slightly at the start and end, and a dry marker shows lengthwise streaks and a feathered edge.

### B4 marker, fix to version A (2026-10-13): the stroke no longer moves while it draws

Owner: A "gets drawn and then while it's drawing it's like switching its position, its axis" while the Rough.js one "just stays in place properly"; fix A first, then compare again.

**Cause, verified by frames and not assumed.** A animated `background-size` from 0 to 100%. The marker SVG stretches to fill that size (its aspect ratio is not preserved), so the whole band was being squeezed and then widened: tilt, angled ends and height all changed during the draw. I paused the animation at fixed times (60ms and 150ms into the first line, and the end) and compared a 100 by 90 pixel crop of the revealed left part of the stroke with the final frame. Old approach: the stroke's bounding box was 100x50 at +21 against 100x48 at +24 in the final frame, and the pixel difference to the final frame was RMSE 2426 (0.037) at 60ms and 1430 (0.022) at 150ms. The stroke really did change shape and position.

**Fix.** The stroke is a `::before` pseudo-element on each line span, at its final size and position from the first frame (background 100% by 100%, `top`/`bottom` -9%), and only a `clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0)` to `inset(0)` reveals it, left to right, 600ms on `--ease-trilsy`. Clipping a pseudo-element, not the span, so the headline text is never hidden. New approach, same measurement: bounding box 100x48 at +24 at every time, RMSE 0 against the final frame at both 60ms and 150ms, so the stroke is pixel-identical to its final form from the first revealed pixel. That also shows the turbulence filter does not re-seed or shimmer during the reveal (the clip does not change the filter region, and the crops are identical frame to frame).
**Other checks.** Line by line at 390px, sampling the two lines' clip values: at 500ms line 1 is 87% revealed and line 2 is 0%; at 1000ms line 1 is done and line 2 is 34%; by 1700ms both are done (delays 300ms and 950ms). Reduced motion (Chrome with `--force-prefers-reduced-motion`): the full static stroke is there at once with no animation. Per-line spans and the wrap logic are unchanged. Not checked: Safari and Firefox (left to the owner; I did not capture the desktop).

### Characters, SVG pack check (2026-10-14)

The owner had GPT-6 reproduce the approved cast as an SVG pack and said it is "kind of broken". Checked at `.../editorial-doodle-pack-faithful/` (pack left untouched; a working copy of what is needed is in `refs/characters-owner/svg-pack/`).
- **Assembly is correct.** Overlaying the 238 part files in manifest order is pixel-identical to the pack's whole-character SVGs (RMSE 0 over all twelve), and the characters look like the sheet they were traced from.
- **The pack traced a different source in slot 5.** Its `source/approved-original.png` has a man with a brown quiff in slot 5 where the approved cast sheet (`cast-sheet-v2-approved.png`) has the grey-haired woman in the white skirt, my professor; slot 5 differs by 21 percent against 3 to 5 percent for the others (slot 4 is 12 percent, from a small offset, not a different person). So the professor is missing from the pack: `05-mustard-quiff` is a different character. Needs the owner: regenerate slot 5 from the approved sheet, or accept a man as the professor.
- **The "parts" are not isolated shapes.** Every one of the 18 to 24 part files per character contains the same 12 complete traced paths, each clipped to a region (that is why the pack is 51 MB). Plotting the clip regions (`svg-pack/analysis/clips.png`) shows them as a partition of the whole 280 by 520 canvas into large bands and blobs (for example hair owns the whole top band, torso and sleeves own big areas across the figure, shoes own the bottom corners), not outlines of the parts themselves. Assembled, they are exact; but moving, hiding or recolouring a part also moves or hides whatever neighbouring artwork falls in its region (hiding the torso in character 06 takes the crossed arms' area with it). Repairing that properly means splitting the traced paths by part, which is re-tracing, so I did not do it; the pack's own README says the parts are "clipped vector groups rather than isolated reconstructed anatomical shapes".
- **Size.** The 51 MB is mostly duplicates (the character folder 13 MB, the parts folder 13 MB, the sheet 13 MB, the sprite 13 MB), about 1.1 MB per character. The same 12 paths kept once and with coordinates rounded to one decimal weigh 24 to 46 KB per character (about 400 KB for all twelve, about 126 KB gzipped), measured, and render within 0.7 percent RMSE of the originals (edge anti-aliasing only). Further simplification of the traced curves (the trace is a pixel staircase encoded as curves, up to 477 segments in one path) would shrink it again but changes the line; not done.
- **In the hero.** `prototypes/b2-hero-v9.html` (stills `b2-hero-v9-{lecture,meeting,interview}-1440.png`) uses the optimised SVGs for eleven characters, still marked TODO: character; the professor is the cut-out from the approved sheet because she is not in the pack.
